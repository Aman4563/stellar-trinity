## Title

Primary: **Cryptographically Controlled Certification of Machine-Learning Evaluation Tasks Using Isolated Generation, Audit, and Evidence Domains**. Alternative: Cryptographic Certification of Machine-Learning Evaluation Tasks Through Isolated Role Domains.

## Abstract (proposed)

Systems and methods control certification of executable tasks for evaluating or training machine-learning solvers. Access-controlled author, auditor, and record-keeper execution domains are arranged such that the author and auditor cannot communicate directly. The record-keeper supplies respective deterministic projections that disclose permitted ledger fields while excluding author design intent and auditor-private acceptance information. Candidate-task bytes and a solver registry are frozen under cryptographic digests. An externally authorized execution operator commits to an execution policy before conducting a pilot and signs an attestation binding the frozen task, solver population, trajectory, scorer closure, policy, and execution interval. A complete attestation representation is routed to the auditor while a restricted binding representation is routed to the record-keeper. Each domain independently verifies a common execution identity. A fail-closed controller creates an append-only certification record only after cryptographic alignment and a predeclared difficulty criterion are verified. Certification evidence may expire according to failure-mode-specific validity intervals.

## Apex pillars in independent claims (1, 16, 24)

1. Externally controlled difficulty pilot: frozen task representation, frozen solver registry, pre-execution signed commitment, signed execution evidence, fail-closed certification.
2. Computed sole-channel projections: direct author-auditor communication prevented; each role receives a deterministically computed, field-restricted view.
3. Dual-blind attestation routing: auditor receives the complete attestation; record-keeper receives only binding facts; both independently align to the same execution identity.
4. Integrated triadic architecture claimed as access-controlled execution domains (not named agents or prompts).

## Design-around foreclosures

1. Single-orchestrator collapse: claims use logical "execution domains," security principals, write authority, access-control boundaries - spec must state domains may share hardware while isolated by capabilities/containers/processes/VMs/IFC labels.
2. Fragmented pilot evidence: "signed pilot attestation" = logical cryptographically bound evidence object; claims cover complete and restricted "representations"; spec clarifies fragmentation/redaction/selective disclosure/multi-message encoding does not create a different execution identity; continuation option: separately signed representations sharing a common execution-identity commitment.
3. Metadata leakage through nominal bridge: claim 1 coarsening + claim 14 closed vocabulary + claim 15 named forbidden fields, declared input closure, byte-for-byte third-party recomputation; spec must treat metadata channels, field omission patterns, ordering, and timing as projection outputs subject to the same field policy.
4. Second bundle home: an accused system that keeps a working copy of task bytes at one path and a delivered copy at another would restore the divergence that a single authoritative home makes unrepresentable. Claim 29 recites capture without conditioning on classification and an unclassifiable turn recorded under a residual value; the spec must state that a turn producing no record is a named coverage gap.

Recite unresolvable as a stale condition in the claim itself, and state in the spec that no cached record, prior approval, or signed envelope waives the gate.

## §112 / drafting cautions

Define objectively: "non-invertible" (many-to-one mapping), "external" (credential-issuance and authorization rules), "pure function," "closure," "controlled by neither," "difficulty" (a three-place relation over task, solver population, and time, grounded in item-response theory). The later tranche needs objective definitions in section 8.2 before allowance. A human-originated turn is an input received from outside the run's execution, regardless of content or classification. Model lineage is a recorded equivalence relation over models sharing a base model, training pipeline, or provider derivation, fixed in the trust root rather than inferred at verdict time. A unanimous council requires every seated ballot to agree, with dissent resolving to rejection and an unseatable roster to human sign-off. A stale checkout is behind, diverged, locally modified, or unresolvable relative to the fetched tip. Completion bound and execution envelope respectively mean a per-attempt wall-clock limit and the hardware and execution class against which it is meaningful, both sourced from a digest-bound human-authored representation. A single authoritative bundle location is the one canonical task-byte path, with other locations holding evidence or standing. Base delivery format means the interoperable required layout; private extension means stricter artifacts inside its permitted trees. Take particular care with "model lineage": an undefined lineage term invites a §112(b) indefiniteness challenge, and the definition must be a recorded relation the system evaluates, not a judgment call. Single-actor infringement: spec drafted so one certification-platform operator performs or causes every method step even when a separate operator credential signs the pilot. Functional claiming: pair each result with disclosed algorithms/data structures/access-control operations - projection pseudocode, canonical serialization rules, signature-validation flow, state-transition tables, recovery algorithms. Drawings needed: architecture and sequence diagrams showing access-control boundaries, pre-run signing, asymmetric routing, digest comparison, certification transition, expiry, recovery, plus an invocation-preflight flow covering the currency gate and the feedback-chain walk (FIG. 9) and a sequence diagram of unanimous-council gate discharge (FIG. 10).

Single-actor caution for the later tranche: claim 34 recites a signed approval envelope produced outside the gated domain, and claim 30 recites a fetched upstream tip. Draft both so the certification-platform operator performs or causes every step, exactly as section 8.31 does for the pilot credential. Externality of the approver is a property of identity and lineage disjointness recorded in the trust root, not a requirement that a separate legal entity operate the council, and the fetched tip is a resource the operator's own system resolves rather than a third-party act.

The proposed tranche introduces sixteen terms that section 8.2 must define objectively before any of them appears in an allowed claim. Two govern routing and capacity. "Task lane" is a write-once routing value drawn from a closed set that selects exactly one root beneath which the single authoritative bundle location for that task is written, distinct from every other sense of lane in the specification, and in particular never a parallel worker, a validation pass, an optimization pass, or an authoring pass. "Structural residency ceiling" is a bound on the number of identifiers resident beneath one root that is fixed by the specification and that no grant of the human-authored requirements representation, no computed projection, and no operator argument may raise, as distinguished from a configured quota. Five govern release authority. "Release-eligible qualification value" is a disposition value expressing that local verification is complete while the terminal release value remains unreachable in the minting domain. "Externally enrolled principal" is an identity whose enrollment and role membership are recorded in a trust root that the minting domain cannot write. "Role threshold" is a required count of distinct verified principals holding a named role at a declared evaluation instant, never a count of signatures, with a revoked, expired, or duplicated principal contributing nothing. "Core control set" is a closed enumeration of controls each of which must carry exactly one recorded outcome under one manifest closure digest, such that a missing, failed, skipped, deferred, waived, duplicate, or extra outcome refuses the terminal value. "Governance publisher" is a repository and verifier whose expected identities, revisions, signer policy, trusted-root version, and release policy are fixed outside the candidate tree and are not candidate or operator inputs. Two govern the sabotage classification. "Governance path" is an enumerated set of paths carrying trust roots, signer policy, trusted-root version, and rotation control, membership in which triggers the same-commit rule. "Published history" is the commit sequence a remote already holds, such that rewriting, force-pushing, replacing, or padding it is the operation claim 37 classifies. Four govern bytes and custody. "Software private key" is a signing key whose private material is a file the process reads, with hardware-backed and device-discovered algorithms refused by name before any subprocess or device enumeration. "Canonical byte profile" is one named serialization grammar whose output, including its terminating byte, is the exact digest and signature input, never a reserialization. "Parent project root" is exactly the directory whose direct child is the vendored governing-specification checkout, never an ancestor of that directory. "Canonical project-root path" is the relative form beginning at the parent project root that every authored, persisted, printed, and passed path value uses, with absolute resolution internal to containment checks. Three govern human inputs. "Grant" is a value a human authored in the human-write-only requirements representation and bound by a digest-bound approval. "Grant proposal" is a dated record staging a value an agent received, which binds nothing, authorizes nothing, and is recorded superseded once the human authors or declines the matching grant. "Attested", as opposed to "measured", is a fact recorded from a dated human answer under a closed elicitation schema, never an outcome of an execution. Take particular care with "task lane" and with "role threshold": the first collides with at least four other uses of the word lane in the specification and must be defined by exclusion, and the second invites a §112(b) challenge if it can be read as a signature count.
