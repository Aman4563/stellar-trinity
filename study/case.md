## Opening

On 16 September 2026 a client assessment arrived covering thirty tasks, three hundred and thirty-six rollouts, and ten repositories [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2, 2026-09-16]. Sarvex Jatasra, founder of Ethara AI and the sole maintainer of Trinity [attested: Q1, Q2], faced the assessment nine days after the recorded delivery [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2, delivery 2026-09-07 and assessment 2026-09-16]. The postmortem catalogued reward-integrity defects, old manifests, unshipped harness dependencies, private images, missing grader modules, and an absent judge runner [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md sections 4 and 5, 2026-09-16]. The case relies on that dated account rather than the original assessment, which this checkout does not carry.

The thirty tasks were already pushed [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2, 2026-09-16]. Should Jatasra keep the delivery standing while repairing defects, or withdraw it [attested: Q8]?

## The delivery of 7 September

The Trinity repository had existed since 22 June 2026, and ten parent repositories were created under the EtharaOrion organization between 24 July and 17 August [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2]. On 7 September, between 13:47 and 23:17, all ten `<name>-samples` repositories were created and pushed with thirty tasks, most commit messages reading "update readme" [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2, 2026-09-07]. Between 14 and 16 September the parent root reports were written or rewritten, and on 15 September at 11:05 the argos `VERDICT.md` recorded BLOCK with twelve required instruments inert, followed at 11:17 by an argos-samples push described as adding ten task bundles with eight of eight measured rollouts, and at 11:34 by a hand edit to the argos verdict after the samples pointer moved [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2, 2026-09-15].

Exhibit 3 transcribes the postmortem's last-recorded dispositions, not a reconstructed snapshot before delivery. Eight of the ten parents carry BLOCK, HOLD, STALE, or BROKEN, while the two SHIP verdicts rest on a typed sign-off dated 7 September and an audit outside the layout [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md sections 1 and 3, 2026-09-16]. Its task column includes the later argos push as well as the initial delivery, so its total is not the assessment population [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md sections 2 and 3, 2026-09-16; study/exhibits/delivery_dispositions.json]. The postmortem states that publication was a human act no instrument could refuse and that none of the twenty-seven parent checks read a disposition, compared the samples pointer to an audited digest, or ran on the samples repositories [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, first cause, 2026-09-16].

The postmortem Jatasra wrote in the days that followed ranked the causes plainly. Enforcement had been prose: the register of unenforced obligations already stated that every refusal outside a small set depended on an agent choosing to honor it, that two hundred and seventeen of two hundred and fifty-one scenario rows had never been verified by anything, and that no external pilot had ever run, so SHIP was unreachable by construction [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, second cause, citing DEFERRED.md]. No oracle had ever run through a shipped verifier; the only executing check replayed a recompute script on the host [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, third cause]. The judge had never been in scope because the reliability check returned nothing when its input file was absent, and no parent had that file [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, fourth cause]. The manifests targeted an old Harbor, and twenty-four of thirty failed against every release since the schema change of 27 March 2026 [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, fifth cause]. And the defects had been known: an earlier consolidated feedback report had already catalogued private registries, self-report graders, silent passes, hidden-name grading, blank judge reasoning, and single-party attestation [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, sixth cause; FEEDBACK.md, committed 2026-09-14]. The seventh cause was the one that stung: effort had gone to the meta-layer and the READMEs. Trinity carried ninety-seven commits and five contracts, the samples repositories averaged three commits each, and one README printed a vacuous status on all twenty-four runs and reported a pass percentage anyway [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, seventh cause].

## The pressures on 16 September

Jatasra named three pressures toward the fast path: the client timeline, revenue or contract value, and the sunk cost of thirty tasks [attested: Q10]. He selected five constraints: the resubmission deadline, solo-maintainer capacity, parent-repository dispositions, the doctrine that publication remained human, and client reputation [attested: Q9]. The teaching note records the conflict between the interview's disposition description and the postmortem table rather than treating them as equivalent.

The interview records four courses of action: accept the agent's recommendation, refuse and add doctrine, defer to the deferred-obligations register, or ship a partial fix [attested: Q5]. The register already warned that refusals outside a limited set depended on agents honoring prose [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, 2026-09-16]. The record does not establish the relative time, cost, or sufficiency of those options.

The four options did not settle the keep-or-withdraw decision [attested: Q5, Q8]. The postmortem's resubmission plan called for freezing the parent and samples repositories at the delivered commits so client citations would remain resolvable [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 10, 2026-09-16]. The decision therefore raised an evidence-preservation question alongside the repair question, without establishing that withdrawal necessarily meant deleting the record.

## The decision

Jatasra faced solo-maintainer capacity and a client resubmission deadline [attested: Q2, Q9]. The question remained whether to keep the thirty-task delivery standing or withdraw it [attested: Q8; ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2, 2026-09-16].

The case closes here. The teaching note carries the resolution and the aftermath.
