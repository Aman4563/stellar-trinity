## Synopsis

On 16 September 2026 the founder and sole maintainer of Trinity received a client assessment of a thirty-task delivery pushed nine days earlier across ten samples repositories [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 2, 2026-09-16; attested: Q2, Q6]. The postmortem catalogued reward-integrity, manifest, harness, and dependency defects [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md sections 4 and 5, 2026-09-16]. Its last-recorded disposition table lists eight of ten parents at BLOCK, HOLD, STALE, or BROKEN and two SHIP verdicts resting on typed acceptance or an audit outside the layout, but it includes later activity rather than proving every parent's state at the initial push [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md sections 1 to 3, 2026-09-16]. The postmortem describes the push as lawful because publication remained human and no check read a disposition [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, 2026-09-16]. The case asks whether to keep the delivery standing or withdraw it under the attested deadline and capacity constraints [attested: Q8, Q9].

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish a control that is stated from a control that is enforced, and locate the exact point at which a fail-closed system was passed around rather than through.
2. Analyze the incentive structure that lets a producer's own gate become theater, and evaluate what independence a single party can and cannot manufacture.
3. Weigh the preservation of adverse evidence against the reputational cost of leaving it public, and articulate when withdrawing an artifact destroys the record a remediation depends on.
4. Apply the distinction between a signature that binds bytes and a judgment that binds meaning, and decide what a digest-bound sign-off does and does not guarantee.
5. Formulate a remediation plan that a hostile reviewer could verify from a clean host, and identify which steps a single maintainer cannot discharge alone.

## Assignment questions

1. The push of 7 September was lawful under the contracts as written. Was the failure one of doctrine, of enforcement, or of operator discipline? Defend one answer using the seven ranked causes [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4].
2. Should Jatasra keep the delivery standing or withdraw it? State the decision, then state what the client's citations, the postmortem, and the reputation of Ethara AI each gain or lose under your choice.
3. Of the four options weighed [attested: Q5], which two are compatible with each other and which two are mutually exclusive? Is registering a gap in a deferred-obligations register a decision or a deferral?
4. The two SHIP verdicts rested on a typed sign-off and on an audit of a folder outside the layout [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 3]. What would a digest-bound signature have changed about each, and what would it have left unchanged?
5. A gate the maintainer controls is theater [attested: Q13]. If you accept that objection, what is the smallest institutional change that makes the gate more than theater, and who has to hold the key?
6. The postmortem records rollout volume alongside reward-integrity defects [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md sections 2 and 5, 2026-09-16]. What would students need to inspect before treating trace volume as evidence of completeness or validity?

## Analysis

*Stated versus enforced.* The postmortem quotes the register as saying that two hundred and seventeen of two hundred and fifty-one scenario rows lacked verification and that no external pilot had run [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, 2026-09-16]. It also records twenty-seven parent checks that did not read dispositions, compare samples pointers to audited digests, or run on the samples repositories [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, first cause, 2026-09-16]. Ask students to locate the missing enforcement boundary without assuming that publishing a register proves an operator read it.

*Publication as a human act.* The postmortem states that publication of `samples/` remained human [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 4, 2026-09-16]. Ask students to distinguish publication authority from gate discharge. GAUNTLET may discharge eligible gates where authorized, but publication, patent filing, operational acceptance, and touchstone admission remain human; journal submission also remains human under ENGRAM's study clause [ledger: GAUNTLET.md permanently human rule; ENGRAM.md Phase S study clause; CHANGELOG.md scribe block, 2026-09-17]. The lesson is not to remove the human from publication, but to require machine qualification before that act.

*The independence problem.* The strongest objection the protagonist named to the lesson that publication must pass through a machine gate is that a gate the maintainer controls is theater [attested: Q13]. The paper the project maintains concedes the point in general form: intra-party separation reduces accidental coupling and self-deception but does not manufacture independence, and evidence produced by Trinity alone is inadmissible under the paper's own standard [ledger: paper/main.md Section 4]. The rebuttal has a stated scope. A gate the maintainer controls still changes what a hurried maintainer can do by accident, still produces a signed record a client can inspect, and still forces a bypass to leave a trace that a classifier can name. What it cannot do is prove the maintainer did not bypass it on purpose. The threat model the postmortem wrote says exactly this: if the organization administrator is the adversary, every anchor is theirs, and the residual defenses are client-side re-execution and a transparency log outside the organization [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 6]. The concession narrows the lesson to its honest form: a producer-controlled gate is a discipline, not an attestation, and the attestation has to come from a party the producer does not control.

*Bytes versus judgment.* The protagonist's strongest objection to the lesson that only a digest-bound signature discharges a gate is that digests bind bytes, not judgment [attested: Q14]. The objection is correct and the lesson survives it with a narrowed scope. The levi SHIP verdict rested on a typed acceptance dated 7 September, and the envora SHIP verdict audited a folder outside the layout [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 3]. A digest-bound signature would have made the first verdict attributable to an identified signer over identified bytes and would have made the second impossible to state without naming the bytes it covered. It would not have made either verdict correct. The instructor should drive to the conclusion the paper reaches independently: attestation repairs accountability, not validity, and the validity work belongs to the auditor's checks, not to the signature [ledger: paper/main.md Section 2].

*Preserving a finding.* The protagonist's strongest objection to the lesson that a finding is preserved and never moved out of the scan window by rewriting history is that preserving a false positive misleads forever [attested: Q15]. This objection deserves the most time, because it is where students most often side with the fast path. The contracts' answer, landed on 17 September, is that the finding is suspicion rather than proof of intent, that rewriting or force-pushing history to move it out of the scan window is itself a sabotage attempt, and that the only remedy is an append-only signed remediation record authorized by a distinct externally enrolled principal group that preserves the flagged commits as evidence [ledger: CHANGELOG.md, 2026-09-17]. The rebuttal is that a false positive with a signed remediation record beside it does not mislead; it documents. The concession is that the remedy presumes a second principal group, and a single maintainer cannot supply one, which is why the register still holds the row open [ledger: CHANGELOG.md, 2026-09-17, DEFERRED.md section 10 reference].

*Keep or withdraw.* The interview records four options and a keep-or-withdraw decision [attested: Q5, Q8]. The postmortem's resubmission plan starts by freezing every parent and samples repository at the delivered commit so client citations remain resolvable [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 10, step 1, 2026-09-16]. Ask students whether withdrawal must destroy evidence, or whether preserving cited artifacts could coexist with withdrawing endorsement of the delivery. The record does not establish the access arrangements under either choice.

## The resolution the case withholds

Jatasra kept the delivery standing, hardened the gates, and planned resubmission [attested: Q11]. On 16 September six stdlib-only modules landed under `tools/`, each registered as a parent check: the release verifier, the sabotage classifier, the sentinel ledger, the Harbor pin, the sealed bundle queue, and the gate entry point that mints only BLOCK, HOLD, or SHIP_ELIGIBLE and never final SHIP [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 7; CHANGELOG.md, 2026-09-16]. The three vectors the delivery had used, a hand-edited disposition, a subject moved after audit, and an unreviewed direct push to samples, each acquired a named refusal [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 6]. Signing became software-only, release authority moved into a separate governance publisher, and new dispositions defaulted to a group record under external producer and approver role thresholds [ledger: CHANGELOG.md, 2026-09-16 and 2026-09-17]. On 17 September, after a parent run answered a historical tamper finding by recommending a force-push, the contracts gained the historical sabotage doctrine that forbids exactly that recommendation [ledger: CHANGELOG.md, 2026-09-17]. The full resubmission reached the client on 17 September 2026 [attested: Q12, Q16].

What the resolution did not do is equally instructive. The external quality-controls runner remained absent, so the release step that depends on it fails closed; the cheap-adversary matrix, alternate correct solutions, mutant ladder, and independent judge replay remained registered as deferred; and the postmortem's own ranked list of what is still owed carried items estimated in days and weeks [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md sections 8 and 11; README.md, Delivery integrity]. The gates that landed refuse what the delivery did. They do not yet prove what a delivery should.

## Lessons, each with its strongest objection

1. Publication must pass through a machine gate before the human act. Objection: a gate the maintainer controls is theater [attested: Q13]. Scope: the gate is a discipline and a trace, not an attestation; attestation requires a party the producer does not control.
2. A typed sign-off is not a sign-off; a gate is discharged by a digest-bound signature. Objection: digests bind bytes, not judgment [attested: Q14]. Scope: the signature fixes who signed what; correctness remains the auditor's work.
3. A finding is preserved, never moved out of the scan window by rewriting history. Objection: preserving a false positive misleads forever [attested: Q15]. Scope: a preserved finding with a signed remediation record documents rather than misleads, and the remedy presumes a second principal a solo project does not yet have.
4. Preserve before repairing. The resubmission plan called for freezing repositories at delivered commits [ledger: POSTMORTEM.md section 10, 2026-09-16]. Objection: withdrawal need not delete those commits. Scope: preserve cited evidence without assuming that public availability and release endorsement must remain identical.
